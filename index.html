Economic Overview

Key Strengths

  • • Large natural resource sector
  • • Stable banking system
  • • Strong trade relationship with the United States
  • • Educated workforce and immigration growth
  • • Expanding technology sector

Current Challenges

  • • Housing affordability pressures
  • • High household debt levels
  • • Productivity concerns
  • • Dependence on commodity cycles
  • • Aging infrastructure in some regions

Major Industries

Energy

Oil, natural gas, hydroelectricity, and emerging renewable energy projects remain central to exports and investment.

Finance

Canada's banking sector is internationally regarded as stable and highly regulated.

Technology

AI, software, gaming, and telecommunications continue to grow rapidly in major cities.

Agriculture

Canada is a major exporter of wheat, canola, seafood, and agricultural products.

Trade & Global Position

United States Partnership

The United States is Canada's largest trading partner by far. Cross-border supply chains support manufacturing, energy exports, agriculture, and consumer goods.

Global Trade Agreements

Canada participates in major trade frameworks with Europe, Asia-Pacific economies, and North America.

Exports

Energy, vehicles, minerals, lumber, aerospace, and food products.

Imports

Machinery, electronics, vehicles, industrial equipment, and consumer goods.

Competitive Advantages

Political stability, natural resources, educated workforce, and access to U.S. markets.

Housing & Cost of Living

Major Issue

Housing affordability has become one of the defining economic and political issues in Canada, especially in large metropolitan areas.

Key Questions

  • • Can productivity growth improve?

Conclusion

Canada remains one of the world's advanced economies with strong institutions and major resource advantages, but long-term prosperity will depend on productivity, infrastructure, housing affordability, and economic competitiveness.
